Theorem 7.3.2. Cauchy’s Form of the Remainder.

Suppose \(f\) is a function such that \(f^{(n+1)}(t)\) is continuous on an interval containing \(a\) and \(x\text{.}\) Then
\begin{equation} f(x)-\left(\sum_{j=0}^n\frac{f^{(j)}(a)}{j!}(x-a)^j\right)=\frac{f^{\, (n+1)}(c)}{n!}(x-c)^n(x-a)\tag{7.3.1} \end{equation}
where \(c\) is some number between \(a\) and \(x\text{.}\)
